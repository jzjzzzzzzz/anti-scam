# Authorized educational use

This project is an anti-scam awareness prototype, not an approval to test a school,
company or another person's mailbox. An educational label alone does not make
an activity lawful. Requirements depend on the jurisdiction, institution,
participants and data processing involved.

Before an exercise, record:

1. Written authorization, the accountable organizer and the approving institution.
2. The exact recipient group, dates, sending identity and approved message content.
3. What records are collected, who can access them, the retention/deletion plan,
   and any required participant, guardian or institutional notices or approvals.
4. An accessible reporting/support contact and a process to stop the exercise.
5. A plan for educational debriefing and aggregate reporting.

Do not collect passwords, verification codes, payment details or other account
credentials. Do not introduce credential-entry pages, malware or unapproved
third-party impersonation. Do not disable organization-wide security controls.
Coordinate any necessary narrow exercise rules with the institution's IT team.

The optional request not to tell other participants is time-bounded and must not
prevent reporting concerns to IT, an exercise coordinator or another appropriate
support channel. Do not use a secrecy request to deter reporting actual abuse.

Technical observations are not personal verdicts. Security scanners and link
forwarding can produce visits; repeated campaign links are not necessarily distinct
people. Follow-up email acceptance does not establish inbox placement or reading.
